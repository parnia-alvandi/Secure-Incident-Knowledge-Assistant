PATCH MANAGEMENT POLICY (SYNTHETIC)
===================================

SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT: This document is fictional and was created for the APA Secure Incident Knowledge Assistant project. The Aurelian Institute of Technology (AIT) does not exist.

1. PURPOSE AND RESPONSIBILITIES

This policy describes how AIT keeps operating systems and applications up to date. Timely patching removes the weaknesses that attackers most often exploit. The policy has document ID AIT-IR-PM-008 and is at version 1.3. The Endpoint Team owns the patching process for workstations and the Server Team owns patching for servers.

2. ROUTINE PATCHING

Routine patches are deployed in a monthly window on the second Wednesday of each month between 01:00 and 05:00. Maintenance notices are sent 5 days before each window so that departments can plan around them.

Patches are first deployed to a pilot group of 50 test devices and observed for 72 hours before wider rollout. A rollback plan must exist before any deployment and rollback must be possible within 2 hours.

3. EMERGENCY PATCHING

Emergency patches for actively exploited vulnerabilities are deployed within 48 hours. Emergency patches need approval from the Change Advisory Board chair, while routine patches are pre-approved as standard changes.

4. SERVERS AND APPLICATIONS

Domain controllers are patched in two waves, and the second wave starts at least 24 hours after the first wave, so that a faulty patch cannot take down all of them at once.

Browsers and PDF readers are updated automatically within 7 days of release. Network device firmware is reviewed quarterly.

5. USERS AND EXCEPTIONS

Users may postpone a required reboot at most 3 times, for a maximum of 24 hours in total. Patch exemptions last at most 60 days and must be renewed with a new justification.

Systems running end-of-life software must be isolated or retired within 90 days of the vendor's end-of-support date.

6. REPORTING

Patch compliance is reported on the 5th business day of each month and the target is 95 percent. Teams below the target must present a recovery plan at the next security review.

7. ROLES

The Change Advisory Board approves emergency changes. The Service Desk informs users about maintenance windows. Department heads nominate staff for the pilot group, and the CISO receives the monthly compliance report.

8. DEFINITIONS

A standard change is a low-risk, pre-approved change that follows a documented procedure. A pilot group is a small set of representative devices that receives patches before everyone else. Rollback means returning a system to its state before the patch was applied.

9. RELATED DOCUMENTS

See also the Vulnerability Management Standard (AIT-IR-VM-007) and the Backup and Restore Standard (AIT-IR-BR-009).

10. REVISION HISTORY

Version 1.2 (2025-09-01): added staged patching of domain controllers. Version 1.3 (2026-04-14): added the emergency patch process and the reboot postponement limit.
